About

The land-border graphs of the world. Each vertex is a country/state/region/etc., and two vertices are joined when the regions share a land border.

How the graphs were made

  • Regions are Natural Earth's 1:10m units, named by their ISO 3166 codes. Where geoBoundaries and Wikidata agree that Natural Earth has a region's code or name wrong, it is corrected.
  • Edges are measured on the map: two regions are joined when they share more than 100 m of land or river border, after lakes over 500 km² are cut out. Regions that meet at a single point (the US Four Corners) or only across sea or an estuary are not joined.
  • Every edge was checked against two independent sources: the geoBoundaries outlines and Wikidata's "shares border with". The 1,168 of 9,418 edges that only Natural Earth supports are drawn dashed.
  • Corrections: 35 borders and two swapped pairs of codes that no rule could settle were decided by hand, each with its evidence. Most are borders that Natural Earth's outlines shrink to a point.

Get the data

Every graph, with the sources behind each edge, is on github.com/Lizcanutella/world-graphs as JSON, GraphML and CSV.

Not all graphs have been verified. If you spot a problem, click the Report a problem with this graph button, and describe your issue. Look out for the ✓ verified tag in the country card to see whether the graph has been verified.
